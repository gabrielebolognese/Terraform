# People and their needs

In a world made from Update 32 on, the people of a city want more than air, water, food and power
as the city grows. How well you look after them decides how happy they are, and a happy city
grows faster and works better.

## What people need

Life support comes first, as always: **power, water, oxygen and food**. Without it no one comes,
and people die (see [Cities](cities.md#people)). Above it, a city asks for more as it grows.

**Comfort**, from **500 people**:

| Need | What meets it |
|---|---|
| **Green space** | A **Park** or a **Biosphere** in reach of the homes. Parks need a terraformed world; before that, a Biosphere is the city's green space. |
| **Food variety** | Food grown more than one way: **Greenhouses**, a **Biosphere**, a **Mega Mall**. Two ways are enough. |

**Services**, from **2,000 people**:

| Need | What meets it | Asked from |
|---|---|---|
| **Medical care** | A **Medical Center** in reach of the homes | 2,000 people |
| **Research** | A **Laboratory**, an **Astronomy Observatory** or a **Research Forum** in reach of the homes | 2,000 people |
| **Transport** | A **Station** in reach of the homes | 5,000 people |
| **Shops** | A **Mega Mall** in reach of the homes | 10,000 people |

A city never asks for something before it may build what meets it: transport waits for the 5,000
people a Station needs, and shops for the 10,000 a Mega Mall needs.

## Reach

A Park, a Biosphere, a Medical Center, a Laboratory, an Observatory, a Research Forum, a Station
or a Mega Mall reaches the homes (Habitat Domes and Skyscrapers) whose middle stands in the square
**33 tiles across** centred on it. Each level up widens the square by **6 tiles**, so upgrading a
service is a way to reach more homes.

Reach also goes **over the city's corridors**: people walk them. A service reaches only the homes
joined to it, by corridors or wall to wall, as buildings join a network. Stations joined by a
railway join the corridors round each, so a railway carries people too.

Only a building that is **running** reaches anyone. A Medical Center in a blackout serves no one,
and a home still going up houses no one yet.

A need is as well met as the share of the city's homes it reaches, counted by the people they
house. Food variety is the city's as a whole: food is carried, not walked to.

## Happiness

Every city that houses people has a happiness, from 0% to 100%. **50% is content**: no better and
no worse. A city too small to ask for anything is content.

Each need the city asks for moves its happiness **up when met** and **down when not**. Half met
is neither:

- green space and research: 7.5 points either way;
- food variety: 5 points either way;
- medical care, transport and shops: 10 points either way.

With all six met, a city is 100% happy; with none, 0%.

What happiness does:

- **Growth.** At 100% people come to the city half as fast again; at 0%, at half the speed. It
  changes how fast the homes fill, not how many people they hold. Unhappy people do not leave.
- **Work.** At 100% every building makes 10% more (power, water, oxygen, food, materials, and the
  credits research earns); at 0%, 10% less. What buildings use does not change.

A Research Forum still draws people to its city on top of this.

## Seeing it

- **The city panel** has a **People** section: the city's happiness and what it does to growth and
  work ("Happiness 70%: growth +20%, buildings make +4%."), then one line for each need. A line
  says how many of the homes are in reach, and what is wanted when they are not. A need not yet
  asked says how many people it is asked from.
- **Click a service** to see how many people live in the homes it reaches. At a Station or a Park
  it also says how many people are there now.
- **The city planner** has a **Coverage** row above the map: **Off**, **Green space**, **Medical
  care**, **Research**, **Transport** and **Shops**. Choose one to see the ground its running
  buildings reach (washed green, the buildings themselves white), the homes they reach (green)
  and the homes nothing reaches (red): the gaps to fill.
- **In the city**, people gather on a Station's platforms and stroll a Park's paths: a figure for
  every few people there, more as the service reaches more homes. Skyscraper windows light up as
  the homes fill, as a Habitat Dome's always have.

## Tips

- Plan services among the homes, not in a district of their own: a Medical Center at the edge of
  the city reaches only the homes near that edge.
- **The first comfort need costs the most.** A Biosphere costs 800 materials, and a new city's
  stores hold 400: build a **Materials Depot** first. In a Standard world it then takes about 45
  minutes to build at 1x, so start it before the city reaches 500 people.
- Upgrading a Laboratory or a Medical Center widens its reach as well as its work.
- A city that meets none of its comfort needs still grows, only a little slower once it passes 500
  people.
