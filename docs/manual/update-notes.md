# Update notes

What is new, changed and fixed in each update, newest first.

---

## Update 36 - Settings, keys and accessibility

Set the game up for your computer and for yourself: how much detail a city is drawn with, every
key, how the mouse, trackpad and touch screen behave, the size and colours of the interface, and
how often your world is saved. Colour is never the only way the game tells you something.

**New**
- **Settings** has every setting now, on the title screen and in the game menu: **Sound**,
  **Graphics**, **Controls**, **Interface** and **Saving**. They are yours, kept by this browser for
  every world. See [Settings](settings.md).
- **Keyboard shortcuts**: **Space** to pause, **1** to **4** for the speeds, **W A S D** or the arrow
  keys to move the view (or turn the planet), **=** and **-** to zoom, **O**, **N**, **C** and **E**
  for Objectives, Alerts, Statistics and Earth, **M** for the world map, **B** back to orbit, **P**
  for the city planner, **U** to upgrade the selected building, and **Ctrl+S** to save. Every one is
  listed in Settings and can be changed, two keys to an action. See [Controls](controls.md).
- **Graphics**: hold a city's detail at **High**, **Medium** or **Low**, or leave it on **Auto**;
  keep fewer or more pictures of a city in memory; turn the **Moving details** (blinking lights,
  turning blades, crowds, steam) off. Each level shows what a frame took to draw on your computer.
- **Controls**: the camera's speed, **Edge scrolling** in a city, **Invert zoom**, and a trackpad
  setting where two fingers move the map and a pinch zooms. A pinch zooms on a touch screen and on
  a trackpad, in every view.
- **Interface**: its size, from 75% to 150%; **Red-green** and **Blue-yellow colour-blind safe**
  colours; **Reduced motion** (or follow your system's setting); **High contrast**; and which alerts
  pop up.
- **Saving**: autosave every 15 seconds, 30 seconds, minute or 5 minutes; **Ctrl+S** saves at once
  and says "Saved."; the game menu says when the world was last saved and where your worlds are
  kept.
- **The planner says what is under the pointer**: the building and what it does, the zone, the
  lines laid and planned.

**Changed**
- The planner's map has patterns as well as colours: power lines dashed, railways striped, plans
  dotted, zones outlined, and homes no service reaches hatched. Its legend shows them.
- Land a city must grow into before it can claim it has a dashed edge.
- On the **World map**, each kind of settlement has its own shape as well as its colour: an outpost
  is a small square, a city a round dot, a metropolis a larger diamond, and a settlement lost to the
  sea is crossed out. The mark stays on the city when you zoom in close, and with gold markers on,
  cities and metropolises are still told apart by their shapes.
- **Alerts** says how many are urgent, for example **Alerts (3, 1 urgent)**, in its own words as
  well as in red.
- A milestone in the planet's news says "milestone", as a warning says "warning".
- With motion reduced, going down to a city is a cut rather than a flight, and the planet does not
  turn by itself.
- Your sound settings moved into your settings' own place in this browser; they come with you.

**Fixed**
- Settings had only the sound.
- In a city, **Esc** did not close the game menu.

**Known issues**
- Keys are named as on an English keyboard: on another layout the key in the place of **W** may
  have another letter on it. It is still the key in that place.
- A frame's time is measured in the cities you see after the page opens; reloading starts again.

## Update 35 - Sound and music

Mars has a voice now: music that warms as the planet does, wind that thickens with the air, the hum
of your cities, and a sound for every building placed, finished and upgraded, every train and
rocket, and every alert.

**New**
- **Music** for every phase of the planet, from the cold, sparse **Cold ground** on bare Mars to the
  warm, full **Living world**. When the planet reaches a new phase, the music fades into the new
  piece over about eight seconds. See [Sound and music](sound.md).
- **Ambience**: the wind, louder and deeper as the air thickens and harder in a dust storm; rain,
  once clouds form over open water on a planet above freezing; the sea, once there is one, louder
  for a city near its shore; and in a city, the hum of the running buildings near the middle of your
  view.
- **Effects**: a thump as you place a building, bell notes when one is finished, rising notes for an
  upgrade, the rush of a train going by and the roar of a rocket lifting off. In a city they come
  from where they happen: louder near the middle of your view, and from the left or the right.
- **Sounds for alerts**: two soft notes for an alert that pops up, three quick pairs for an urgent
  one. A soft tick for every button.
- **Sound settings**: **Settings** on the title screen, or **Menu** then **Settings** in a world. A
  slider each for **Master volume**, **Music**, **Ambience**, **Effects** and **Interface**, and
  **Mute all sound**. They are yours, kept for every world you play.

**Changed**
- The game is silent until your first click or key press, as browsers require; then the music
  starts.
- The milestone chime is part of the game's sound now, on the **Interface** slider.
- **Credits** says where the music and sound come from: the game makes them itself.

**Fixed**
- Alerts, events and Earth were silent. An event that needs your choice now sounds as an urgent
  alert; a missed launch window and a failed contract as an alert; a rocket to Earth roars as it
  lifts off.
- The milestone chime could not be turned down or off.

**Known issues**
- Graphics and control settings are not available yet.
- Your sound settings are kept by this browser: another browser, or a world's exported file, does
  not carry them.
- A rocket coming home makes no sound; only its lift-off does.
- A train that stops at a station near the middle of your view is heard as it comes, not again as
  it leaves.

## Update 34 - Earth: trade, shipments and contracts

Earth is part of the game now. Every time Mars and Earth line up, your Spaceports' rockets fly
there with what you sell and come back with what you ordered, and Earth offers contracts that pay
well if you deliver on time.

**New**
- **The Earth button**, beside Statistics and Alerts: your standing with Earth, the next launch
  window, Earth's prices, your orders, the rockets away, contracts, and a record of it all. See
  [Earth: trade, shipments and contracts](earth.md).
- **Launch windows**, about every 9 and a half minutes at 1x (Standard pace; about every 71 seconds
  at Classic). At each one, every city with something to send or fetch flies its Spaceport's
  rocket, the one you see on the pad, and it lands about 3 minutes later. A city whose rocket is
  away on a supply run misses the window, and its orders wait for the next.
- **Buying from Earth**: materials, water, food and oxygen, paid when you order and cancellable
  for a full refund until the rocket goes. Settlers go on sale once you have met a contract, and
  rovers after three.
- **Selling to Earth**: put materials, water, food or oxygen up for sale, and the rocket takes
  them at the next window. A rocket never takes a store below a quarter of its room.
- **A market**: prices move each window with Earth's supply, rise as you buy and fall as you sell,
  and recover between windows. Your reputation makes them better or worse.
- **Contracts**: at each window Earth asks for materials, water or research, due by the third
  window on. Met, it pays well and your reputation rises by 10. Failed, your reputation falls by
  20.
- **Alerts** for a missed window and a failed contract (these pop up), a rocket home and a
  contract met.

**Changed**
- Earth comes to new **Standard** and **Terraformed start** worlds. A **Sandbox** has none, and
  worlds made before this update play as they always have.
- Select a Spaceport to see what its rocket is doing: at home, flying at each launch window, or
  away to Earth.
- The **While you were away** summary says how many of Earth's contracts were met and failed.
- Earth changes nothing if you leave it alone: its offers wait for you to accept them.

**Known issues**
- A contract's materials and water go from whichever cities' rockets fly, in the order the cities
  were founded: you cannot choose which city sends them.
- A city's rocket flies from its first free Spaceport; a second Spaceport only helps when the first
  one's rocket is away.
- At Classic pace a window, and an offer, come about every 71 seconds.
- The statistics do not chart your trade with Earth or your reputation.
- Earth makes no sound yet.

## Update 33 - Events that ask for decisions

Mars now throws problems at your cities, and the odd piece of luck: a meteor shower, a machine
about to fail, a sickness, Earth's supply ship, a find in the rocks. Each gives you a few choices
with their costs, and about three minutes to choose. Do nothing, and the worst happens.

**New**
- **Events**, from about half an hour into a new world, about one every 25 minutes at 1x, each on
  one of your cities. See [Events and decisions](events.md) for every event and its choices.
  - **Meteor shower**: take shelter (no materials or research for a minute), raise the shields
    (150 credits), or keep working and take 3 hits. Each hit takes a building down a level and
    wrecks one at level 1.
  - **A machine failing**: repair it (materials), or run it at half its output for 10 minutes.
    Left alone, it breaks down for 10 minutes.
  - **Outbreak** in a city of 200 or more: Medical Centers shelter people from it. Treat everyone
    (credits) or quarantine the city.
  - **Earth's supply ship**, early, late or lost, with materials and settlers aboard.
  - **A discovery**: study it for research, or sell the samples to Earth.
- **The event card** at the top left: what happened and where, the time left, each choice with
  its cost and what it does, and what happens if you do nothing. A choice you cannot pay for says
  why.
- **Alerts**: an event waiting for you pops up as urgent and stays under **Now** until you choose.
  When it happens, **What happened** says what you chose and what it did.
- **Event history** in **Alerts**: every event your world has had, decided or missed. It is kept
  with the world.
- A city's status line says when its people are under cover or in quarantine, and a building with
  a fault says so when you select it.

**Changed**
- Events come to new **Standard** and **Terraformed start** worlds. A **Sandbox** has none, and
  worlds made before this update play as they always have.
- The **While you were away** summary says how many events came while you were gone and how many
  nobody answered.

**Known issues**
- Events keep coming while the game is closed, and nobody answers them: each comes to its worst.
  Ten hours away can bring about twenty.
- A supply ship's materials are lost when the city's stores are full, which a growing city's
  usually are. Its settlers land if there are empty homes.
- Earth does not yet ask anything of you: requests from Earth come with trade.
- Events make no sound yet.

## Update 32 - People, not a number

The people of your cities now want more than air and food as their city grows. Look after them
and they are happier: a happy city fills its homes faster and every building in it works better.

**New**
- **Needs beyond life support.** From 500 people a city wants green space (a Park or a Biosphere)
  and food grown more than one way. From 2,000 it wants medical care and research. From 5,000 it
  wants transport (a Station), and from 10,000 shops (a Mega Mall). A city is never asked for
  something before it may build what meets it.
- **Reach.** A service meets a need only for the homes near it: those in the square 33 tiles
  across centred on it, 6 tiles wider for every level, on the same corridors. It must be running.
- **Happiness**, 0% to 100%, 50% content. Each need met raises it and each need not met lowers
  it. At 100% a city grows half as fast again and its buildings make 10% more; at 0% it grows at
  half the speed and they make 10% less.
- **People** on the city panel: the city's happiness and each need, how much of the city it
  reaches, and what would meet it.
- **Coverage** in the city planner: choose a service to see the homes it reaches in green and the
  gaps in red.
- **People in the city.** Crowds gather on a Station's platforms and stroll a Park's paths, more
  as the service reaches more homes. Click a service to see how many people it reaches.

**Changed**
- In a new world, the status line says "Life support is met" when power, water, oxygen and food
  are all supplied, since there is now more to need.
- Skyscraper windows light up as the city's homes fill, as a Habitat Dome's do.
- The planner's **Overview** lists the city's happiness and each need.
- **Terraformed start** and the **Example planet**: the ready-made cities now have parks, medical
  centers, laboratories, stations and malls among their homes, and somewhat fewer people for it.
- Worlds made before this update play as they always have: their people need only life support.

**Known issues**
- In a Terraformed start, Stations reach only some of a city's homes: a Station stands beside the
  railway, and the ready-made cities have only a few lines.
- Making a Terraformed start, and building the Example planet, take longer than before: about
  40 seconds each, where they took about 20.
- Happiness is not yet shown on the world map's city cards, in the Settlements list, in Alerts or
  in Statistics.

## Update 31 - Knowing what is happening

The game now tells you when something needs you and takes you straight there, and keeps charts of
how your planet and every city have done.

**New**
- **Alerts.** A blackout, a city short of water, oxygen, food or materials, buildings that cannot
  run (and why), and a dust storm on its way pop up at the top right as they happen. Urgent ones
  (a blackout, people going without) come first and stay longer. Click one to go there: down to
  the city, with the building selected and in the middle of the view.
- **The Alerts button**, beside **Menu**, counts what needs you now and turns red while something
  is urgent. It lists everything that holds now, and everything that happened since you opened the
  world: builds and upgrades finished, objectives done. Click any line to go there.
- **Grouped:** many buildings offline for one reason in one city are one line, for example
  "12 Greenhouses offline in Ares".
- **Statistics**, beside **Alerts**: charts of the planet (temperature, pressure, oxygen, ocean,
  biomass, carbon dioxide, progress, credits and people) over the whole game, and of each city
  over its last 150 years, including what it made and used of each resource and its power balance.
- **Each city at a glance.** The Settlements list and the world map show each city's people
  against its homes, anything running short, and what is being built.

**Changed**
- The city planner's **Charts** tab adds what the city made and used of each resource.
- The planet now keeps its own record in your save. A world made before this update starts its
  record the first time you open it.

**Known issues**
- What happened is kept only until you leave the world: it starts fresh each time you open it.
- Alerts make no sound yet.
- On a very large world, with dozens of cities, an alert can come a few seconds late.
- In a city, the pop-up messages can cover the Objectives panel while it is open.
- A new city shows "Spaceport offline" until power reaches it.

## Update 30 - Your first hour

A new Standard world now teaches you to play by doing, one step at a time, and there is always a
next goal to reach, with a reward worth having when you get there.

**New**
- **The guide.** In a new Standard world, a card at the top of the screen takes you through seven
  first steps: found a city, place power, place a dome, connect them, raise a building a level,
  claim land, and lay a railway to a second settlement. The button or card to press glows. The
  guide follows what is really in your world, so however you do a step, it counts. **Skip the
  guide** puts it away for good.
- **Objectives.** Press **Objectives** for three lists of goals - your city, the planet and the
  network - each showing exactly what counts, how far along you are, and the reward. Press
  **Claim** when a goal is done, and the next one opens. The planet's warming goals count from
  where your planet started, so their bars begin nearly empty.
- **Rewards:** settlers from Earth, rovers that stay for good, materials, credits, gold markers for
  your cities, and the right to found a **metropolis**, which you could not do before.
- **Milestones.** A new phase, a new city, a great structure finished or an objective done brings
  up a card, a moment of light round the screen and a short chime.

**Changed**
- **Found a metropolis** appears in **Settlements** once "A city of 1,000" is claimed, and in every
  Sandbox world.
- Worlds made before this update, Sandbox worlds and Terraformed starts have no guide and no
  objectives.

**Known issues**
- Claiming land, the guide's sixth step, waits for 200 people, so the guide takes about 40 minutes
  to an hour to finish.
- The chime plays at a fixed, quiet volume; there is no way to turn it off until sound settings
  arrive.
- A reward of settlers or materials is lost in part when the city has no empty homes or no room in
  its stores. Claim when there is space.
- The bar for "The caps let go" moves with the weather: the goal is only 3 K from the start, and
  a dust storm can take the bar back for a while.

## Update 29 - Vehicles that use the city

Rovers and trains no longer pass through buildings. Rovers drive real routes round your city, and
the route sets how long a job takes. Trains run on a track each way and stop at stations.

**New**
- **Rovers drive round the city.** A rover leaves by the Headquarters' garage door and drives
  over corridors and open ground to its job. It never goes under a building, over a crag or up
  ground too steep to build on. It turns where its way turns and keeps behind another rover on the
  same stretch.
- **Corridors are roads.** A rover drives along a corridor twice as fast as over open ground.
- **The route sets the trip.** Each tile of open ground on the way takes a rover about half a
  second at 1x, out and again home, on top of the work itself.
- **A track each way.** Each way of a city railway has its own track, drawn as a double track
  that curves round bends. Trains ride the curve car by car and slow down for bends. They stop for
  about 4 seconds at a Station's platform and at the end of a line, then run back.
- **Maglevs and freight.** A railway that runs past a Station carries white maglevs. One that runs
  past none carries freight: an orange locomotive pulling wagons stacked with containers.

**Changed**
- **New rules for placing buildings,** in worlds made from this update on:
  - a building no rover can get to is refused ("no rover can get there from the garage");
  - so is one that would wall other buildings in, and the message says how many;
  - the ground just in front of the garage door is kept clear;
  - nothing can go up on a rover's way until that rover is home again.
- A rock, a patch of ground to level, or an upgrade that no rover can get to is refused the same
  way.
- In a city packed edge to edge, rovers drive long ways round, so builds there take longer than
  before. Leave gaps between buildings, or lay corridors.
- Worlds made before this update keep their old trip times and placing rules, but their rovers are
  drawn driving round the buildings too.

**Fixed**
- Rovers and trains no longer drive through buildings.
- A rover or train passing behind a building is now hidden by it, not drawn over it.
- Trains going opposite ways along a line no longer pass through each other.

**Known issues**
- Trains on different lines still pass through each other where the lines cross.
- A rover driving past another one at work beside its site can pass through it.
- Levelling ground can leave the tiles beside it too steep to build on, with no rock on them.
  Laying a corridor, cable or railway there tells you to send a rover to break the crag, but there
  is none: use **Level ground** on that tile instead.

## Update 28 - Time that means something

Building now takes real time. Small structures still go up in seconds, but bigger ones and higher
levels take minutes and then hours, and the work carries on while the game is closed.

**New**
- **Real build times.** From about 20 seconds for a Storage Depot to an hour for a Mega Mall at
  level 1, and longer at every level: a Mega Mall at its top level takes about 8 hours.
- **The Standard pace** for new worlds: the planet itself changes more slowly, over days of play,
  while your cities keep their quick rhythm. A supply rocket's trip is still about a minute.
- **The Classic pace**, the fast game from before, is still there when you make a world.
- **The build queue.** When every rover is busy, new builds and upgrades wait in line, already
  paid for, and a rover takes the next one when it is free. Reorder them with **Up** and **Down**,
  or **Cancel** for a full refund. More Rover Posts mean more builds at once.
- **Countdowns everywhere:** above every worksite, in the city's **Builds** list, in the building
  panel, on the **Upgrade** button, and on the build cards.
- **Builds finish while you are away**, up to 10 hours, and the **While you were away** summary
  says what your cities finished.

**Changed**
- Speeds at the Standard pace are **pause, 1x, 2x and 3x**. Sandbox worlds have every speed.
- Every world made before this update plays at the Classic pace, exactly as before.
- Building with every rover out now queues the job instead of refusing it.

**Known issues**
- An upgrade costs as much as a new building, so early on it is usually better to build new.

## Update 27 - The front door

The game now has a title screen and keeps as many worlds as you like.

**New**
- **A title screen** with Continue, Worlds, New world, Example planet, Settings and Credits.
- **Worlds**, each with its own save: play, rename, duplicate, export, import and delete them.
- **New world** with a name, a **seed** (the same seed always makes the same planet), and three
  modes: **Standard**, **Sandbox** (unlimited everything) and **Terraformed start** (a living
  planet with cities already built).
- **A game menu** (Menu or Esc) to save, or save and quit to the title screen.

**Changed**
- Your planet from before this update is kept as a world called "My planet".

**Fixed**
- A world whose save cannot be read is kept as it is and never saved over.
