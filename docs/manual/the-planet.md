# The planet

The view from orbit is where you change Mars itself. Drag to turn the planet and scroll to zoom.
The panel on the left holds everything you need, from top to bottom.

## The top line

- **Phase and year.** Which phase the planet has reached, with a line saying what it means, and
  the current year. At the Standard pace a planet year passes in about four and a half minutes at
  1x.
- **Credits.** Your bank, and how much it grows each year: income minus the upkeep of your
  machines. Research in your cities adds more on top.
- **Speed.** **pause**, **1x**, **2x** and **3x** at the Standard pace; **pause**, **1x**,
  **10x**, **100x** and **1000x** at the Classic pace; every speed in Sandbox.

Important news, such as a new phase, appears in a banner for a few seconds. Messages such as
"Cannot order: ..." tell you why an order was refused.

## The phases

Mars changes in six steps. Once the planet reaches a phase it keeps it.

| Phase | Name | Reached when |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Barren | The start: about -60 °C, with air less than a hundredth as thick as Earth's |
| 1 | Warming | You deploy your first mirror or greenhouse gas |
| 2 | Runaway thickening | The planet warms past about -57 °C and the frozen carbon dioxide starts to boil off |
| 3 | First water | It is above freezing and the air is thick enough for liquid water |
| 4 | Ecopoiesis | Life has taken hold |
| 5 | Oxygenation and buffer | There is real oxygen and nitrogen in the air |
| 6 | Living world | All six minimums are met at once: warm enough, thick enough air, breathable oxygen, little carbon dioxide, oceans covering 30-70% of the planet, and a thriving biosphere |

At the Standard pace, Phase 1 comes within seconds and Phase 2 after about an hour and a half of
play at 1x. The later phases take many hours each; a full terraforming takes several days of play
at 1x (a third of that at 3x).

**Progress toward Earth-like** shows how close the planet is to Earth itself, and the line below
it says how many of the six Living world minimums are met and what is still short. A Living world
reads about 87%: the last stretch to a truly Earth-like planet goes beyond it.

There is no ending yet. Reaching Living world is the goal today; victory, scores and achievements
arrive in a later update.

## What the planet needs next

This box gives one piece of advice: the thing holding the planet back right now, why, and what to
do about it, with a button to do it (**Order one** or **Seed the biosphere**). It is marked
**needs you** when only you can fix it, and **in progress** when what you ordered is already
working. If you cannot afford the advice yet, it says how many credits you need.

## Build: the planet's machines

Each machine is ordered with **+1** (the button shows the price) and dismantled with **-1**.
**Switch off** pauses a machine without losing your order.

- Orders come online gradually, about one unit every nine minutes of each kind at 1x, not all at
  once.
- Each extra unit of the same kind costs 6% more than the last.
- Every unit costs a little upkeep each year. Dismantling is free but refunds nothing.
- You can have up to 50 units of each kind.

| Machine | Price of the first | What it does | Unlocks at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orbital mirror array | 240 credits | Reflects more sunlight onto Mars and warms it | Phase 0 |
| Solar shade | 260 credits | Blocks some sunlight and cools the planet, if it overheats | Phase 0 |
| Greenhouse factory | 300 credits | Releases powerful greenhouse gases that warm the planet strongly; they fade over time | Phase 0 |
| Atmospheric processor | 520 credits | Releases carbon dioxide locked in the soil into the air, thickening it; the supply runs out eventually | Phase 1 |
| Comet redirect | 1,400 credits | Steers icy comets onto Mars to bring it water | Phase 2 |
| Nitrogen import | 900 credits | Brings nitrogen, the gas that makes air thick and safe to breathe | Phase 4 |
| Carbon scrubber | 700 credits | Removes carbon dioxide from the air for the final breathable mix; it eases off before the air gets too thin | Phase 5 |
| Orbital magnetic shield | 1,800 credits | Protects the atmosphere from being stripped away by the solar wind | Phase 5 |

A machine that is not unlocked yet says when it arrives, for example "Comet redirect is not
unlocked yet - it arrives at phase 2".

### Seeding life

**Cyanobacteria seeding** is a one-time action that brings the first life to Mars. It becomes
possible once there is liquid water, the planet is between about 5 °C and 40 °C, and the air is
thick enough. It costs nothing. The row says **not yet possible**, **ready** or **seeded**.

## Credits

You start with 3,000 credits. The planet pays you a steady income that grows as Mars becomes more
habitable, and your cities' Laboratories, Astronomy Observatories and Research Forums earn
research credits on top. Machines cost credits to order and a little each year to run.

## Target bands

Six bars show where the planet stands against what it needs: **Temperature**, **Pressure**,
**Oxygen**, **Ocean cover**, **Biosphere** and **Carbon dioxide**. Each says *below minimum*,
*habitable*, *at target* or *above band* (carbon dioxide says *at target*, *safe* or *too high*).
Hover over the marks on a bar to see the minimum and the target.

## Weather and the sky

Mars has moods of its own:

- **Dust storms** rise every hour or so of play at 1x and last from ten minutes to over half an
  hour. They darken the sky and cool the planet for a while.
- **Comet impacts** are rare, roughly once a day of play. They deliver water and a burst of warmth.
- **The Sun** brightens and dims a little over long cycles.

You cannot stop these; they are reported in the **Log**, along with milestones such as the first
liquid water and breathable air.

## Settlements

The **Settlements** section lists your cities and outposts. **Found a city** and **Found an
outpost** (and **Found a metropolis**, once unlocked) start a new one: type a name first if you
like, then click the planet. **Open** takes you down to one, and **World map** shows the whole
planet. See [Cities](cities.md).

Each city's line in the list also says how it is doing: its people against its homes, anything
running short, and what is being built, for example "120 of 160 people - short of water - 2 going
up, 1 waiting for a rover". See [Alerts and statistics](alerts-and-statistics.md).

## Instruments

The **Instruments** button (top right) opens a detailed drawer for the curious: every number the
planet runs on, charts, and a way to replay the planet's history. It also shows the
**While you were away** summary when you come back to a world.

**Careful:** the **Reset planet** button in Instruments starts your planet over at once, with no
confirmation, and the world is saved that way.
