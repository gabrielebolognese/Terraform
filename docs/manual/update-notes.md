# Update notes

What is new, changed and fixed in each update, newest first.

---

## Update 32 - People, not a number

The people of your cities now want more than air and food as their city grows. Look after them
and they are happier: a happy city fills its homes faster and every building in it works better.

**New**
- **Needs beyond life support.** From 500 people a city wants green space (a Park or a Biosphere)
  and food grown more than one way. From 2,000 it wants medical care and research. From 5,000 it
  wants transport (a Station), and from 10,000 shops (a Mega Mall). A city is never asked for
  something before it may build what meets it.
- **Reach.** A service meets a need only for the homes near it: those in the square 33 tiles
  across centred on it, 6 tiles wider for every level, on the same corridors. It must be running.
- **Happiness**, 0% to 100%, 50% content. Each need met raises it and each need not met lowers
  it. At 100% a city grows half as fast again and its buildings make 10% more; at 0% it grows at
  half the speed and they make 10% less.
- **People** on the city panel: the city's happiness and each need, how much of the city it
  reaches, and what would meet it.
- **Coverage** in the city planner: choose a service to see the homes it reaches in green and the
  gaps in red.
- **People in the city.** Crowds gather on a Station's platforms and stroll a Park's paths, more
  as the service reaches more homes. Click a service to see how many people it reaches.

**Changed**
- In a new world, the status line says "Life support is met" when power, water, oxygen and food
  are all supplied, since there is now more to need.
- Skyscraper windows light up as the city's homes fill, as a Habitat Dome's do.
- The planner's **Overview** lists the city's happiness and each need.
- **Terraformed start:** the ready-made cities now have parks, medical centers, laboratories,
  stations and malls among their homes, and somewhat fewer people for it.
- Worlds made before this update play as they always have: their people need only life support.

**Known issues**
- In a Terraformed start, Stations reach only some of a city's homes: a Station stands beside the
  railway, and the ready-made cities have only a few lines.
- Making a Terraformed start takes longer than before: about 45 seconds, where it took about 20.
- Happiness is not yet shown on the world map's city cards, in the Settlements list, in Alerts or
  in Statistics.

## Update 31 - Knowing what is happening

The game now tells you when something needs you and takes you straight there, and keeps charts of
how your planet and every city have done.

**New**
- **Alerts.** A blackout, a city short of water, oxygen, food or materials, buildings that cannot
  run (and why), and a dust storm on its way pop up at the top right as they happen. Urgent ones
  (a blackout, people going without) come first and stay longer. Click one to go there: down to
  the city, with the building selected and in the middle of the view.
- **The Alerts button**, beside **Menu**, counts what needs you now and turns red while something
  is urgent. It lists everything that holds now, and everything that happened since you opened the
  world: builds and upgrades finished, objectives done. Click any line to go there.
- **Grouped:** many buildings offline for one reason in one city are one line, for example
  "12 Greenhouses offline in Ares".
- **Statistics**, beside **Alerts**: charts of the planet (temperature, pressure, oxygen, ocean,
  biomass, carbon dioxide, progress, credits and people) over the whole game, and of each city
  over its last 150 years, including what it made and used of each resource and its power balance.
- **Each city at a glance.** The Settlements list and the world map show each city's people
  against its homes, anything running short, and what is being built.

**Changed**
- The city planner's **Charts** tab adds what the city made and used of each resource.
- The planet now keeps its own record in your save. A world made before this update starts its
  record the first time you open it.

**Known issues**
- What happened is kept only until you leave the world: it starts fresh each time you open it.
- Alerts make no sound yet.
- On a very large world, with dozens of cities, an alert can come a few seconds late.
- In a city, the pop-up messages can cover the Objectives panel while it is open.
- A new city shows "Spaceport offline" until power reaches it.

## Update 30 - Your first hour

A new Standard world now teaches you to play by doing, one step at a time, and there is always a
next goal to reach, with a reward worth having when you get there.

**New**
- **The guide.** In a new Standard world, a card at the top of the screen takes you through seven
  first steps: found a city, place power, place a dome, connect them, raise a building a level,
  claim land, and lay a railway to a second settlement. The button or card to press glows. The
  guide follows what is really in your world, so however you do a step, it counts. **Skip the
  guide** puts it away for good.
- **Objectives.** Press **Objectives** for three lists of goals - your city, the planet and the
  network - each showing exactly what counts, how far along you are, and the reward. Press
  **Claim** when a goal is done, and the next one opens. The planet's warming goals count from
  where your planet started, so their bars begin nearly empty.
- **Rewards:** settlers from Earth, rovers that stay for good, materials, credits, gold markers for
  your cities, and the right to found a **metropolis**, which you could not do before.
- **Milestones.** A new phase, a new city, a great structure finished or an objective done brings
  up a card, a moment of light round the screen and a short chime.

**Changed**
- **Found a metropolis** appears in **Settlements** once "A city of 1,000" is claimed, and in every
  Sandbox world.
- Worlds made before this update, Sandbox worlds and Terraformed starts have no guide and no
  objectives.

**Known issues**
- Claiming land, the guide's sixth step, waits for 200 people, so the guide takes about 40 minutes
  to an hour to finish.
- The chime plays at a fixed, quiet volume; there is no way to turn it off until sound settings
  arrive.
- A reward of settlers or materials is lost in part when the city has no empty homes or no room in
  its stores. Claim when there is space.
- The bar for "The caps let go" moves with the weather: the goal is only 3 K from the start, and
  a dust storm can take the bar back for a while.

## Update 29 - Vehicles that use the city

Rovers and trains no longer pass through buildings. Rovers drive real routes round your city, and
the route sets how long a job takes. Trains run on a track each way and stop at stations.

**New**
- **Rovers drive round the city.** A rover leaves by the Headquarters' garage door and drives
  over corridors and open ground to its job. It never goes under a building, over a crag or up
  ground too steep to build on. It turns where its way turns and keeps behind another rover on the
  same stretch.
- **Corridors are roads.** A rover drives along a corridor twice as fast as over open ground.
- **The route sets the trip.** Each tile of open ground on the way takes a rover about half a
  second at 1x, out and again home, on top of the work itself.
- **A track each way.** Each way of a city railway has its own track, drawn as a double track
  that curves round bends. Trains ride the curve car by car and slow down for bends. They stop for
  about 4 seconds at a Station's platform and at the end of a line, then run back.
- **Maglevs and freight.** A railway that runs past a Station carries white maglevs. One that runs
  past none carries freight: an orange locomotive pulling wagons stacked with containers.

**Changed**
- **New rules for placing buildings,** in worlds made from this update on:
  - a building no rover can get to is refused ("no rover can get there from the garage");
  - so is one that would wall other buildings in, and the message says how many;
  - the ground just in front of the garage door is kept clear;
  - nothing can go up on a rover's way until that rover is home again.
- A rock, a patch of ground to level, or an upgrade that no rover can get to is refused the same
  way.
- In a city packed edge to edge, rovers drive long ways round, so builds there take longer than
  before. Leave gaps between buildings, or lay corridors.
- Worlds made before this update keep their old trip times and placing rules, but their rovers are
  drawn driving round the buildings too.

**Fixed**
- Rovers and trains no longer drive through buildings.
- A rover or train passing behind a building is now hidden by it, not drawn over it.
- Trains going opposite ways along a line no longer pass through each other.

**Known issues**
- Trains on different lines still pass through each other where the lines cross.
- A rover driving past another one at work beside its site can pass through it.
- Levelling ground can leave the tiles beside it too steep to build on, with no rock on them.
  Laying a corridor, cable or railway there tells you to send a rover to break the crag, but there
  is none: use **Level ground** on that tile instead.

## Update 28 - Time that means something

Building now takes real time. Small structures still go up in seconds, but bigger ones and higher
levels take minutes and then hours, and the work carries on while the game is closed.

**New**
- **Real build times.** From about 20 seconds for a Storage Depot to an hour for a Mega Mall at
  level 1, and longer at every level: a Mega Mall at its top level takes about 8 hours.
- **The Standard pace** for new worlds: the planet itself changes more slowly, over days of play,
  while your cities keep their quick rhythm. A supply rocket's trip is still about a minute.
- **The Classic pace**, the fast game from before, is still there when you make a world.
- **The build queue.** When every rover is busy, new builds and upgrades wait in line, already
  paid for, and a rover takes the next one when it is free. Reorder them with **Up** and **Down**,
  or **Cancel** for a full refund. More Rover Posts mean more builds at once.
- **Countdowns everywhere:** above every worksite, in the city's **Builds** list, in the building
  panel, on the **Upgrade** button, and on the build cards.
- **Builds finish while you are away**, up to 10 hours, and the **While you were away** summary
  says what your cities finished.

**Changed**
- Speeds at the Standard pace are **pause, 1x, 2x and 3x**. Sandbox worlds have every speed.
- Every world made before this update plays at the Classic pace, exactly as before.
- Building with every rover out now queues the job instead of refusing it.

**Known issues**
- An upgrade costs as much as a new building, so early on it is usually better to build new.

## Update 27 - The front door

The game now has a title screen and keeps as many worlds as you like.

**New**
- **A title screen** with Continue, Worlds, New world, Example planet, Settings and Credits.
- **Worlds**, each with its own save: play, rename, duplicate, export, import and delete them.
- **New world** with a name, a **seed** (the same seed always makes the same planet), and three
  modes: **Standard**, **Sandbox** (unlimited everything) and **Terraformed start** (a living
  planet with cities already built).
- **A game menu** (Menu or Esc) to save, or save and quit to the title screen.

**Changed**
- Your planet from before this update is kept as a world called "My planet".

**Fixed**
- A world whose save cannot be read is kept as it is and never saved over.

## Update 26 - Structures with character

**Changed**
- Every building is drawn with its own details, varied a little from one to the next, so cities
  look built rather than stamped out.
